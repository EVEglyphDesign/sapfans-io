Working in repositories

Practitioners increasingly keep their working knowledge in a Git repository rather than a folder of documents. If you have not worked this way, this is what it means and why people on the board will ask you for one.

One subject, one repository

A programme, a product area, a method. Everything on that subject in one place, versioned, with a history of what changed and when.

Provenance on every input

Each document records where it came from — SAP Note, Help Portal page, workshop, extract. Six months later you can still defend the number.

Readable by an AI surface

Point Claude or Perplexity at the repository and it works from your record instead of guessing. This is the practical reason the format is spreading.

Portable between employers

Your method travels with you. The client's data does not — that stays in the client's estate, never in a repository you control.

Exchanging with other practitioners

  1. Find the person, not the fileSomeone posts about a model, a mapping or a method you need. The useful move is to contact them, not to hunt for a download.
  2. Reach them on LinkedIn or WhatsAppMost people list a contact in their post. Say which post you're coming from and what specifically you want — vague requests get ignored here as anywhere.
  3. Ask for read access, not a copyIf the repository is private, ask to be added as a read-only collaborator. The owner keeps control, sees who has access, and can revoke it. An emailed zip has none of those properties.
  4. ReciprocateThe people who get the most out of an exchange like this have something of their own to open up. Publish the method; keep the client material out of it.
One rule, no exceptions. Client-identifiable material never goes into a shared repository or onto this board — not anonymised, not redacted, not "just the structure". If you are unsure whether something qualifies, it qualifies.
